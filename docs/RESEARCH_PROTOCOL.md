# TinniRelief Research Protocol

## Purpose
Evaluate usability, preference and self-reported experience without making unsupported claims of tinnitus treatment efficacy.

## Research boundary
The application may collect structured self-reports such as:
- sound preference
- comfort
- annoyance
- session completion
- perceived relaxation
- sleep-related reflection

These are user-reported experience measures, not diagnostic measurements.

## Suggested study structure
Before data collection:
1. Define the research question and outcomes.
2. Obtain appropriate ethics/governance approval.
3. Use de-identified study identifiers.
4. Freeze the software version and sound library.
5. Predefine analysis rules and missing-data handling.

## Preference analysis
For each sound or soundscape, summarize:
- number of exposures
- comfort rating
- annoyance rating
- preference/selection frequency
- session completion

Do not convert preference data into claims of clinical efficacy.

## Safety
Define stop criteria and a process for handling reports of worsening symptoms or concerning symptoms. The application should continue to direct users with red-flag symptoms toward appropriate clinical evaluation.

## Reproducibility
Record:
- software commit SHA
- sound-library/version identifier
- browser/device context where relevant
- session protocol
- export schema version
- analysis script/version

## Evidence boundary
A positive usability or preference result does not demonstrate that TinniRelief treats tinnitus or changes clinical outcomes.
